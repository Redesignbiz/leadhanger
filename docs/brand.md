# LeadHanger — brand architecture

LeadHanger is a standalone product brand. This document is its own definition,
derived from what the site actually says rather than from any parent company's
material. When a question comes up about positioning, wording, proof, or visual
treatment for LeadHanger, this file is the authority.

That has one hard consequence worth stating up front, because getting it wrong
is how this document came to be written: **Redesign Business is the operator,
not the brand.** Its consultancy positioning, its service pillars, its buyer
personas, its outreach sequences and its visual language govern Redesign's own
communication. None of them govern LeadHanger. Reaching for them because they
are the nearest available document is the specific mistake this file exists to
prevent.

---

## 1. Relationship to the operator

| | |
|---|---|
| Architecture | Standalone product brand |
| Name in all contexts | LeadHanger. Never "LeadHanger by Redesign" |
| Where the operator appears | Footer only, as "Built and operated by Redesign Business" |
| What the operator lends | Legal entity, contact address, hosting and support |
| What the operator does not lend | Its client roster, its case studies, its service model, its palette, its typographic system, its market positioning |

LeadHanger earns its own credibility. Borrowing the operator's enterprise logos
would make the product read as agency output, which is the opposite of what a
standalone architecture is for.

### Explicitly not inherited

Do not carry any of these onto a LeadHanger surface, in copy or in design:

- The 100-day delivery discipline, or any service-timeline promise framed as a
  methodology
- "Six practices", CX / UX / PD / BD / AI practice language
- Consulting buyer personas (VP CX, CPO, CDO at enterprise accounts)
- The cold-outreach messaging sequence, which is built for email from a
  consultancy and does not describe how someone reads a product page
- The operator's client names, logos, or named case studies
- The operator's visual language: warm near-black ground, terracotta accent,
  2px radius, the wordmark treatment

## 2. Category and buyer

**Category.** Lead qualification and distribution for dealer networks. The
comparison set is lead management and conversational marketing tooling, not
consulting and not general-purpose CRM.

**Buyer.** A brand that reaches end customers through a dealer, agent, or
installer network, and the person inside it who is accountable both for digital
demand and for what the channel does with it. In practice: the marketing or
digital channel owner, the sales director who owns the network, or the CRM owner.

**What the buyer already believes.** That their site produces interest, and that
the interest is being wasted downstream. They do not need to be taught that the
handoff is broken. They need to be shown that it is fixable and measurable.

---

## 3. The qualifier

The sharpest line LeadHanger has, and the one that decides whether a visitor is
in the audience:

> The purchase cannot happen in a checkout.

Bought once every five or ten years. Fitted, installed, or serviced by a
professional. Researched by a buyer who does not know the category well. Those
visitors do not fill in forms, they leave.

This is the qualifier, not the industry list. Categories (tires and auto
service, HVAC, kitchen and bath, insurance and agency networks, batteries and
energy, appliances and home improvement) are illustrations of the shape. Lead
with the shape; the list is support. A vertical outside the list still qualifies
if the purchase has that shape.

---

## 4. The one visible problem

> The marketing is working. The handoff is not.

Three failure modes, and no more than three:

1. Nothing on the dealer side to receive the lead. It arrives as email in a
   shared inbox, unowned and unprioritized.
2. Response measured in days. The intent that made someone a lead has expired by
   the time anyone calls.
3. No line of sight after the handoff. Marketing can prove it produced demand.
   Nobody can prove what the network did with it.

---

## 5. The offer

A conversation instead of a form, qualification and consent inside that
conversation, real-time routing to a specific dealer, and follow-through that is
recorded. Four steps, in that order.

Three surfaces, each belonging to a different group of people. This split is
load-bearing: the buyer's real question is what their dealers would have to use.

| Surface | Whose | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Conversation on your site | Your visitors | One line of script, structured dialogue, consent captured in the flow |
| App for dealers and agents | Your network | Today's leads with call times, reminders, one-touch calling, outcome capture |
| Dashboard for the brand | Your team | Volume and funnel, response time and conversion by dealer and region |

## 6. Proof policy

**No customer is named. Not yet.**

Outcome figures are stated as platform outcomes, unattributed:

- 32% lead-to-sale ratio across the dealer network
- 40% faster dealer response to digital leads
- 50% qualified lead ratio across the platform
- 10,000+ parties worldwide operating on LeadHanger

Category-level references are permitted and useful: "built for a tire
manufacturer's dealer network" says where the product was hardened without
claiming a logo. Naming a brand requires that brand's written clearance, and
until one exists the answer is the category, not the name.

**Never publishable, in any form:** internal operational counts from a client
deployment. They measure different things over different windows than the
published ratios above, and putting them near each other invites a question with
no good answer.

**No unsourced statistics.** Any figure that is not in the list above does not go
on a LeadHanger surface. Archived marketing drafts carry percentages like
"increases submissions by 25%" with no source behind them. They are not usable.

## 7. Commercial model

Three service levels: Lite, Pro, Custom. Setup is ready-to-use, about three
weeks, and about ten weeks respectively.

**Prices are not published.** Scope drives the number, so the number follows a
scoping conversation. The plan comparison exists so a buyer can locate
themselves, not so they can self-serve.

**Say no in the comparison.** Lite genuinely lacks dealer follow-up tracking,
customer follow-up, CRM integration and CX operations, and the table says
"None" in those cells. A comparison that never says no is not a comparison, and
a buyer who discovers the gap after signing feels sold to.

**One call to action, everywhere: book a demo.** Twenty minutes, walking the flow
end to end. Not a trial, not a download, not a whitepaper.

## 8. Voice

Senior, plain, and concrete. The reader is a practitioner who has been pitched
before.

**Rules**

- One visible problem, one offer, one call to action. Nothing extra.
- Short declaratives. Say the thing, then stop.
- Describe behaviour, not benefit. "The dealer marks the outcome" beats
  "improves visibility".
- State limits in the open. "No system is perfect, and we do not claim
  otherwise" is on the site on purpose.
- No dashes joining clauses in headings or body copy. Compound words are fine
  (white-label, lead-to-sale, self-service). Use a period, a comma, or a colon.
- Numbers are figures, set in tabular numerals. Never spelled out in copy.

**Never**

Transform your business · end-to-end solution · holistic · strategic partnership
· AI-powered without saying what it does · seamless · leverage · unlock ·
revolutionary · exclamation marks · "we would be happy to" (write "worth a
call?")

**Calibration.** These three lines are the voice, and are all live on the site:

> The marketing is working. The handoff is not.

> By the time a dealer follows up, the customer has already bought elsewhere.

> If your customers buy from you through someone else, the handoff is the part
> worth fixing. The vertical matters less than the network.

## 9. Visual identity

Defined in `src/app/globals.css` and `src/app/layout.tsx`, on LeadHanger's
corporate palette and typeface. It no longer tracks the Yeni Müşterim project,
and Redesign's own visual language (warm near-black ground, terracotta accent,
2px radius, its wordmark treatment) still does not apply here, per section 1.

**Corporate palette.** Dark-first. These are the brand's values, used verbatim:

| Role | Token | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Page ground | `ground` | `#0B101B` |
| Deep ground (footer) | `ground-deep` | `#080B12` |
| Panel | `panel` | `#151A24` |
| Raised panel | `panel-high` | `#21262F` |
| Border | `line` | `#282E3B` |
| Primary blue (fills, buttons) | `blue` | `#144EE4` |
| Bright blue | `blue-bright` | `#3686FE` |
| Light blue | `blue-light` | `#579EE4` |
| Pale blue (blue type, highlights) | `blue-pale` | `#85C8FF` |
| Primary text | `ink` | `#F5F6F8` |
| Secondary text | `ink-muted` | `#C0D3EA` |

**Derived values.** A handful of roles have no corporate value. Each is marked
"derived" in the stylesheet with its reason: a third text step for labels
(`ink-dim`), a stronger border (`line-strong`), a blue-cast panel
(`panel-blue`), a deeper blue for the Results section (`blue-deep`), a darker
button hover (`blue-hover`, because white on `blue-bright` fails contrast), and
muted text and rules on the blue section (`on-blue-muted`, `on-blue-line`). Sage and rose remain as state
colours for good and lost outcomes; the corporate palette has no equivalent.

**Blue is a fill, not type.** `#144EE4` is 2.9:1 on the ground. Blue text uses
`blue-pale` or `blue-bright`.

**No shadows.** This is the load-bearing rule. Depth is a 1px border plus tonal
layering, which is why no shadow scale exists in the tokens. A `shadow-*`
utility on a LeadHanger surface is a mistake, not a variation.

**Type.** Inter, one face for everything. Headlines carry tight tracking
(-0.025em to -0.035em). Section labels uppercase at 11px, 600, 0.12em. Figures
that must align use `tabular-nums`, not a monospaced face.

**One blue section per page, maximum.** Results sits on solid `blue-deep`
(`#11389E`, derived). Flat corporate `blue` across a full section was too bright
against the dark page. A second blue section and neither reads as emphasis.

**Logo.** The client-supplied lock-up at `public/images/leadhanger-logo.svg`
has a dark wordmark (`#161A25`), so the site serves
`public/images/leadhanger-logo-on-dark.svg`, a recolour onto corporate values:
mark in `#85C8FF` and `#3686FE`, wordmark in `#F5F6F8`. The wordmark is drawn as
paths, so it does not follow the ink token.

## 10. Not decided yet

- An official light logo variant. The one in use is a recolour, not a
  client-supplied file.
- A square, higher-resolution favicon master. The supplied one is 32×23.
- Whether the demo call to action becomes a scheduling link or a form. It is a
  `mailto:` today.
- Whether any customer will be cleared for naming, and on what terms.
- Whether analytics is added. If it is, the privacy notice's "no tracking"
  section becomes false.
- Consent wording for US outbound calling. The platform captures consent and
  passes the record with the lead. What a dealer needs before dialling varies by
  state and contact method, and that answer belongs to the client's counsel.

## 11. Where the current site disagrees with this document

Recorded rather than silently fixed, because the site is not to be changed
without a decision.

**A customer is named, in two places.** `src/components/Results.tsx` line 25 and
line 42 print `site.caseStudy.client`, which resolves to a brand name, and line
42 links out to that brand's case study on the operator's website. Section 6 of
this document says no customer is named until one is cleared. The rest of the
site is already compliant: `Results.tsx` line 22 and `Faq.tsx` line 33 both say
"a tire manufacturer" without a name.

Resolving it is one edit to `site.caseStudy` plus the sentence that consumes it.
Not done here, pending a decision.

**The page's section order carries an invented rationale.** The comment at the
top of `src/app/page.tsx` explains the order as a chain of buyer questions. That
chain has no source. It was written to justify a layout after the fact and
should be replaced with the reasoning in sections 3 through 7 of this document,
which the order does in fact follow: qualify the reader, name the problem, show
the mechanism, then prove it.

**Two sections open on the same idea.** `Problem` opens with products that
"cannot be bought in a checkout" and `Industries` opens with "the purchase that
cannot happen in a checkout". Per section 3, that is one idea, and section 8's
"nothing extra" says it should appear once.
